Drainage Problems

A better driveway starts with controlling water.

The exact solution depends on the driveway shape, slope, surrounding terrain, existing ditches or culverts, and where the water can safely go. Practical improvements may involve reshaping the driveway, restoring roadside drainage, or correcting specific problem areas.

01

Driveway Crowning

Reshape the travel surface so water is encouraged to move off the driveway instead of following it downhill.

02

Ditches and Swales

Restore or reshape roadside drainage paths that have filled in, eroded, or stopped carrying water effectively.

03

Low Areas

Correct locations where water collects and repeatedly softens the driveway or creates potholes.

04

Culvert Areas

Clean and reshape accessible areas around existing culverts when debris or poor grading is interfering with flow.

Planning the Work

Drainage work should match the terrain.

Hocking County driveways often cross sloping ground and receive runoff from wooded hillsides. A useful repair needs to consider where the water is coming from, where it is currently going, and what happens during heavier storms.

MacLeod focuses on practical driveway and site improvements that can be completed with grading, stone, skid-steer, or mini-excavator equipment.

  • Identify visible water paths and recurring damage
  • Reshape the driveway to shed water where practical
  • Restore roadside ditches and swales
  • Correct eroded or frequently washed-out areas
  • Coordinate stone and grading with the drainage work
Engineered drainage design, surveying, permitting, utility relocation, and work within regulated streams or waterways are not included unless specifically arranged.

Frequently Asked Questions

Helpful answers before requesting a quote.

Why do potholes keep returning in the same place?
Repeated potholes often develop where water collects, follows the wheel path, or softens the underlying material. Repairing the surface without addressing the water may only provide a temporary improvement.
Can you clean or reshape driveway ditches?
Yes, accessible roadside ditches and swales may be cleaned or reshaped when that work fits the site and equipment.
Do you install new culverts?
Culvert-related work depends on the location, size, drainage conditions, utilities, permitting, and whether the work affects a regulated waterway. It must be reviewed and specifically included in the agreed scope.
Can you stop water from running down a steep driveway?
Grading, crowning, turnouts, ditches, or other practical measures may reduce the amount of water following the driveway, but the best approach depends on the slope and available drainage paths.
Is this engineered drainage design?
No. MacLeod provides practical construction work based on visible site conditions and experience. Engineering, surveying, drainage calculations, and permitting are separate services when required.
