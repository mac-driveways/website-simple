Planning the Work

The right scope depends on access and site conditions.

A light excavation project should be clearly defined before work begins. Equipment access, slopes, buried utilities, overhead clearance, soil conditions, disposal needs, and the desired finished result can all affect the plan.

Photos, approximate measurements, and a clear explanation of the goal help determine whether the work is a good fit for the available equipment.

  • Review access for skid-steer or mini-excavator equipment
  • Identify visible utilities, slopes, and obstacles
  • Define what material will be moved or removed
  • Clarify the desired grade or finished condition
  • Coordinate hauling, stone, or additional equipment if needed
Utility locating, surveying, engineering, environmental permitting, regulated-waterway work, major tree removal, blasting, and large-scale excavation are not included unless specifically arranged.

Frequently Asked Questions

Helpful answers before requesting a quote.

What counts as light excavation?
Light excavation generally includes smaller, defined projects suited to skid-steer or mini-excavator equipment rather than major earthmoving, deep excavation, or large commercial site work.
Can you move and spread gravel or soil?
Yes, accessible material movement and spreading may be included when the amount, site conditions, and equipment needs fit the project.
Do you perform drainage ditch work?
Practical driveway ditches, swales, and drainage shaping may be included. Engineered systems, permitting, and regulated-waterway work must be separately reviewed.
Do I need utilities marked?
Any project involving digging should account for buried utilities and private lines. Utility locating and the responsibility for identifying private facilities should be addressed before excavation begins.
Can you haul debris away?
Hauling or disposal may be available depending on the material, volume, destination, truck access, and agreed scope. Describe the material clearly when requesting a quote.
