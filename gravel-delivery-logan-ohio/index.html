Delivery Options

Dump Delivery

Planning the Work

Planning a delivery around real site conditions.

The right plan depends on more than the number of tons. Driveway width, overhead clearance, turnaround space, slope, soft areas, and current road conditions can all affect how material can be delivered.

Sending photos and a clear location before scheduling helps determine whether a standard dump, tailgate spreading, or delivery with equipment is the best fit.

  • Confirm the job location and delivery distance
  • Discuss the driveway or site access
  • Estimate the quantity and delivery approach
  • Coordinate spreading or grading when needed
  • Schedule around material and site availability
Material availability and pricing depend on the source, quantity, delivery distance, truck access, and current site and road conditions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Helpful answers before requesting a quote.

Do you deliver gravel directly to homes and rural properties?
Yes. Delivery is available for driveways, lanes, parking areas, cabin properties, and other accessible rural sites around Logan and Hocking County.
Can you spread the gravel instead of leaving one pile?
Depending on the driveway and site conditions, tailgate spreading or delivery with grading may be available. Access and placement should be discussed before scheduling.
How do I know how much gravel I need?
Send the approximate length, width, problem areas, photos, and a description of the intended improvement. MacLeod can help think through a practical quantity and delivery plan.
What affects the delivery price?
The material source, quantity, delivery distance, truck access, current road conditions, and whether spreading or grading is included all affect pricing.
What should I tell you about access?
Mention narrow entrances, steep slopes, low branches or wires, soft ground, limited turnaround space, gates, bridges, culverts, or other conditions that may affect a loaded dump truck.
